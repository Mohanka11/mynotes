4. Electronic Mail (E-mail)

Definition: An easy and fast way of sending and receiving messages from one computer to another anywhere in the world.

Snail Mail: A term used for traditional postal mail due to its slow speed compared to e-mail, which takes only a few seconds to reach its destination.

Advantages of E-mail

  • Cost-effective and fast.
  • Reaches any part of the world within seconds.
  • Accommodates messages of any length (from a few lines to thousands of lines).
  • The same message can be sent to multiple people at no extra cost.

Requirements & Programs

  • Requirements: To operate an e-mail account, you need an Internet connection, an e-mail program, and the e-mail ID of the receiver.
  • E-mail Programs: Can be free or paid. Users must register to receive an account (e-mail ID and password) and are responsible for account security. Examples include www.gmail.com, www.hotmail.com, and www.yahoomail.com.
Account Rules:
The e-mail account name is fixed and cannot be changed, but you can change your password.

5. Steps to Compose and Send an E-mail

  1. Open the e-mail website (e.g., www.gmail.com) and click Sign In.
  2. Enter your username and click Next.
  3. Enter your password and click Next.
  4. Click the + Compose button on the left pane to open the New Message window.
  5. Enter the receiver's e-mail ID in the 'To' box.
  6. Enter the Subject of the e-mail.
  7. Type the content of your message.
  8. Click the Send button.

6. Netiquettes (Internet Etiquette)

General Behavior

Be tolerant and careful because people online come from many different places and have different views. Be open to listening and do not jump to conclusions.

Social Networking Netiquettes

  • Screen name: Do not include personal details like your last name or date of birth.
  • Personal information: Do not share private details (passwords, full name, home address, school name, phone numbers, e-mail ID, birth date) without parent's permission.
  • Photographs and videos: Do not post them online without getting parental permission.
  • Online friends: Do not agree to meet an online friend unless you have parent's permission.

E-mail Netiquettes

  • Computer courtesy: Be courteous and respectful while sending e-mails or chatting.
  • Do not shout: Avoid typing messages in all uppercase (capital) letters, as this is considered shouting.
  • Language issues: Avoid using offensive comments and never hurt someone's feelings.
  • Check spellings and grammar: Ensure your messages are free of spelling and grammatical errors.
