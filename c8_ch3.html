Robotics & Automation Summary

Comprehensive Overview & Key Concepts

1 Introduction to Robots and Robotics

  • Origin of the Word: Originates from the Slavonic word ‘robota’, which translates to work or labour. p. 35
  • Definition of a Robot: A machine programmed to carry out a complex series of tasks, mimicking or copying human actions to make human labor easier and quicker. p. 35
  • Definition of Robotics: The branch of science, engineering, and technology that combines to design and build human-like machines capable of sensing, thinking, and acting according to algorithms. p. 38, 39
  • Historical Fact: Genghis, a six-legged, insect-like robot, was developed in 1980 by roboticist Rodney Brooks at MIT. p. 35

2 Advantages and Real-World Applications

Robots automate repetitive, highly precise, or dangerous tasks across multiple domains: p. 36, 38

  • Homes: Smart appliances like the Roomba are used for automated house cleaning. p. 35
  • Travel: Autonomous, self-driving cars manufactured by companies such as Tesla, BMW, and Volkswagen, operated via AI systems. p. 36
  • Healthcare: High-precision surgery assistance, manufacturing artificial limbs, generating test reports, and elderly care (e.g., Toyota Healthcare assistant helps patients regain walking ability). p. 36
  • Manufacturing Assembly & Packing: High-speed, high-accuracy assembly of complex parts (e.g., SCARA robots designed for pick-and-place/assembly operations). p. 36
  • Logistics: Automated warehouse storage and transit (e.g., drones handling shipping and goods delivery). p. 36
  • Hotel Industry: Front desk management, information sharing, storage, and check-in/out via voice and facial recognition (e.g., the Botlr robot). p. 36

3 Types of Robots

Five primary classifications: p. 36, 39

  • Pre-Programmed Robots: Automatic machines designed for repetitive tasks, often replacing humans in hazardous environments (e.g., robotic firefighting systems that analyze, locate, and conduct search-and-rescue operations). p. 36
  • Humanoid Robots p. 36, 39
  • Autonomous Robots p. 36, 39
  • Teleoperated Robots p. 36, 39
  • Augmented Robots p. 36, 39

4 How Robots Work: The "Sense-Think-Act" Principle

Key Distinction: While all robots are machines, not all machines qualify as robots. p. 38, 39

A robot relies on a continuous three-step loop to function: p. 38

1. Sense

Gathers environmental data using sensors like cameras (sight), microphones (sound), and touch sensors (texture/handling). p. 38

2. Think

Analyzes input data to recognize patterns and compare scenarios using pre-programmed algorithms. p. 38

3. Act

Executes calculated decisions using internal motors and actuators. p. 38

5 Key Characteristics & Limitations

  • Human Dependency: Robots must always be operated and given commands by humans. p. 38
  • No Human Replacement: Because they rely entirely on human commands and algorithms, robots cannot replace humans.
